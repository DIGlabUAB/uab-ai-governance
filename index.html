About Us

What We Do

The UAB Medicine AI Governance Program supports the AI in Medicine & Healthcare Committee (AIM-HC) by coordinating the evaluation of AI technologies, maintaining UAB Medicine’s Central AI Inventory, establishing standards for validation and ongoing monitoring, defining appropriate human-in-the-loop checkpoints, and managing changes throughout the AI lifecycle.

Our Why

We believe AI can make health care better, and we believe in the people bold enough to recognize that innovation and governance belong in the same room. We understand that every algorithm we govern touches a person: someone’s diagnosis, someone’s timing, someone’s outcome. While some may see review boards and standards, we see the reason a clinician, a team member, and a patient can trust what happens next.

Our Goal

To make UAB Medicine a place where responsible innovation thrives and where clinicians, patients, and teams can embrace AI with confidence, supported by meaningful human oversight and a shared commitment to safety, equity, trust, and ethical practice.

AIM-HC Oversight

Governance structure, roles, and the review process

The program supports responsible AI development, evaluation, adoption, and use involving UAB Medicine Enterprise data, systems, infrastructure, or licensed AI tools, or intended for patient care or operations at UAB Medicine.

The process applies whether AI is developed internally, obtained from a vendor, or enabled within an existing platform. Research-only work within this scope also starts with intake and may qualify for Expedited Review.

Committee & Subcommittee

AIM-HC Committee

The AI in Medicine & Healthcare Committee (AIM-HC) is UAB Medicine’s primary multidisciplinary body for AI governance and serves as the review body for AI use cases assigned to Full Review. It brings AI expertise and leadership to one table for the responsible adoption of AI across UAB Medicine Enterprise.

AIM-HC Subcommittee

The AIM-HC Subcommittee is the core team that runs AIM-HC’s day-to-day operations and serves as the review body for AI use cases assigned to Focused Review.

Every AI use case has a named person who answers for it, from the first request until the work ends or is formally transferred. How many accountable roles you name depends on the review pathway (see Who to Name under Oversight Process).

AI Executive Sponsor

The leader who champions the use case, such as a department chair, service line director, or C-suite executive. This person confirms it fits institutional priorities, secures funding and staff for as long as it runs, puts a Business or Clinical Sponsor and a Model Owner in place, and sees that any conditions of approval are met.

Business or Clinical Sponsor

A permanent faculty or staff member who speaks for the use case: what it is for, who it affects, and the workflow it fits into. This person is responsible for a complete and accurate intake form, prepares the workflow and the people who will use the AI, keeps the use within what was approved, reports problems, and tells the program before the use changes or expands. When the AI comes from a vendor, this person obtains the vendor’s transparency information and specifications for the review.

Model Owner

The person who answers for the technical side of the AI, whether UAB Medicine built it, bought it, or turned it on in an existing platform. This person is responsible for how the AI is built or set up, tested, documented, and maintained, and supports security review, validation, and monitoring. The role starts as soon as AI is being built or tested, including in research, when that work is within the program’s scope.

Initial Registration and Triage

Every AI use case within the program’s scope comes in the front door through the AI intake form. The AI Governance Program triages the request and routes it to one of three review pathways.

The intake identifies the intended AI use case, the named accountable role, technical need, and proposed activities. The program first determines whether the request qualifies for Expedited Review. For requests requiring an Adoption Stage, routing depends on the requested stage and whether the AI use case is clinical or administrative/operational.

Three Review Pathways

1

Expedited Review

Available Tool within approved uses, or research-only work.

2

Focused Review

Any request at Development; administrative/operational requests at Pilot or Production.

3

Full Review

Clinical requests at Pilot or Production.

The UAB Medicine AI Governance Program considers both what an AI use case is intended to accomplish, and the evidence needed to support its use through defined Adoption Stages and Decision Gates.

For AI-Enabled Solutions assigned to an Adoption Stage, the process evaluates the suitability of the proposed use, readiness for real-world implementation, evidence of impact, and continued performance. Each Adoption Stage authorizes specific activities within documented approval.

Each Decision Gate determines whether an AI-Enabled Solution may enter the requested stage, what activities are authorized, and what conditions must be met.

Each Decision Gate ends in one of four outcomes:

Approved Approved with Conditions Deferred Declined
